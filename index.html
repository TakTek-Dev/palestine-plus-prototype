01

One more line

The client’s name already holds the brief. “Palestine” is the news; “+” is what a reader gets for one more second of attention. The identity draws that literally.

A news line is a horizontal stroke: you read along it. The plus is that line with a second one standing across it. The standing line is not read yet; it is waiting.

Press it and it falls. It lies down beneath the first line, and you are one layer deeper: the context under the fact, the numbers under the context, the voices, then what happens next.

So the mark is not a decoration on the name. It is the button the whole site is built from. Every story is a line with standing lines across it, and the reader decides how many to lay down.

Closed: there is more. Open: one line deeper.

02

The mark is drawn from the type

The plus is not typed. A font’s + sits on the maths axis with square ends; ours is measured off Newsreader itself so it belongs to the letters beside it.

  • Bars

    Two equal bars with round ends, each 0.62 of the stem of Newsreader’s l at the wordmark’s weight (600, display size). Thin enough to read as a line, not a sign.

  • Arms

    0.98 of the x-height, so the plus stands exactly as tall as the lowercase it follows.

  • Crossing

    At the middle of the x-height: the lying bar continues the reading line of “Palestine”.

  • Two colours

    The lying bar is ink, the same matter as the line you are reading. The standing bar is green: the one more line. Drawn standing bar first, lying bar over it.

04

The plus as grammar

The mark runs the interface. Every state of a story is a state of the plus.

  • Standing

    There is more under this line. It is always a button, never decoration.

  • Leaning

    On hover the standing bar tips 9° towards the line, as if about to fall.

  • Laid down

    The bar has fallen under the first line: this layer is open. Two lines, one under the other.

  • 3
    The count

    Beside a headline, the number of layers under it. A reader sees the depth before going in.

05

Colour is depth

Every layer is one tint deeper than the one above it, cool greys tinted towards the ink, until the last layer is the ink itself. Nothing is beige; nothing is warm paper.

  • paper#FFFFFFThe fact. Line one sits on white.
  • l1#F2F5F8Context: one layer down.
  • l2#E5EBF1Numbers: two down.
  • l3#D2DBE4Voices: three down.
  • ink#0B1420What next, and the type itself.
  • plus#0F9D58The standing bar in marks and icons on light. Never text.
  • plus-t#087A43Green as text and badges on white: 5.4:1.
  • plus-on#35D48AThe standing bar and badges on ink: 9.6:1.
  • mute#4F5D6EMeta, sources and labels: 6.2:1 on white.

Green is the only colour, and it means one thing: one more line. The flag’s green, kept for the plus, the +n count and the ways in. No red, no alarm colour: the facts carry their own weight.

08

Motion: laying the line

Movement follows the idea: lines are drawn left to right, standing bars fall, layers open downward. Nothing bounces for its own sake.

  • The loader1.8 s · first page of a session

    Whichever page a reader lands on first: a line is drawn across the screen, a green bar stands up through it, then falls to lie beneath it, and the page opens along the line. Replay it.

  • Page to page340 ms close · 600 ms open

    Leaving a page, a line is drawn across it and the two halves close on it; the next page opens along the same line. If it is slow to arrive, a green segment runs along the line. Back and forward never land on a closed page.

  • The fall500 ms · cubic-bezier(.5,0,.25,1.25)

    The standing bar rotates down about its foot and settles a hair past flat, like a ruler laid on a desk.

  • Opening a layer550 ms · ease-out (.22,1,.36,1)

    The layer’s height grows from nothing, its text arrives 120 ms later. “Open every layer” lays them one after another, 110 ms apart, top to bottom.

  • Lines on scroll1 s · from the left

    Each section rule is drawn from its left end as it enters the screen.

  • Headline hover450 ms

    A line is laid under the headline, from the first letter.

  • Reduced motionprefers-reduced-motion

    No loader, no drawing; layers open instantly. Everything still works.

09

Photographs: the real place

Most stories carry a photograph, and every one shows the place it names, captioned with where and when it was taken.

Photographs come from Wikimedia Commons under free licences, shown in their own colour, never tinted or filtered. A photograph of a different town is never used to stand in for the one in the story.

Where no honest photograph exists, the story gets a plate: the place name on ruled lines, the plus above it. It is better to show nothing than to show something false.

10

Voice: write the line first

Every story must stand as one sentence before anything is laid under it.

  • Write

    Settlers set fire to two homes in Duma overnight, the village council says.

  • Not

    Horror in Duma as violence erupts once again.

  • Write

    No date has been set for phase two to begin.

  • Not

    The future remains uncertain.

  • Rules

    Who, what, where, and who says so, in the first line. Numbers with a date and a counter. Quotes short and exact. British spelling. Sentence-case headlines. No adjectives the facts don’t need.

11

Misuse

A plus is a common shape; these four mistakes turn it into something else.

  • A solid green plus on a tile

    Reads as a pharmacy or first-aid sign. The plus is always two colours.

  • A longer lower arm

    Turns the plus into a Latin cross. The four arms are always equal.

  • Turned 45°

    An × means close, delete, wrong. The plus never turns except to lie down.

  • Typed from a font

    A typed + sits on the maths axis with square ends. The drawn plus crosses at the lowercase middle.
