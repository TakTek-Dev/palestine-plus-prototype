Gaza · Aid60s

What counts as “dual-use”, and who decides?

  1. 0–15s
    What it is. Civilian goods Israel says could also serve a military purpose. This winter the list catches tents and tarpaulins.
  2. 15–30s
    Who decides. Israel’s COGAT, the defence ministry body that runs crossings, keeps the list and approves each shipment.
  3. 30–45s
    What it means. A tarpaulin is treated like a controlled item: it waits for its slot, and its one day a week, at the crossing.
  4. 45–60s
    Can it be challenged? Agencies can resubmit a rejected item, case by case; the UN’s OCHA tracks denials and delays.
Sources: NRC, OCHA, COGATWatch it
Gaza · Places60s

What is Al-Mawasi?

  1. 0–15s
    The place. A strip of sandy coast west of Khan Younis and Rafah, once farmland and greenhouses.
  2. 15–30s
    The order. During the war Israel’s army declared it a “humanitarian zone” and told families to move there.
  3. 30–45s
    The camp. It became a tent city that can be seen from the air, with little water or sanitation.
  4. 45–60s
    The winter. 950,000 people in Gaza live in sites at risk of flooding (NRC); 162,000 tents are out of use (PalInfo).
Sources: NRC, PalInfo, UNRWAWatch it
West Bank60s

Areas A, B and C: why the map decides the harvest

  1. 0–15s
    The split. The 1995 Oslo II accord divided the West Bank into Areas A, B and C, with different civil and security control.
  2. 15–30s
    Area B. Palestinians run civil affairs but Israel holds security, so the army can close land to its owners.
  3. 30–45s
    The groves. About 220,000 to 230,000 dunums of olive land sit behind the wall or near settlements (PalInfo).
  4. 45–60s
    In October. For those groves, reaching a tree can need a permit, a coordinated date, or an escort.
Sources: Oslo II text, PalInfoWatch it
